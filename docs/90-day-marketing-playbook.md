# Cataloo — 90-Day Zero-Budget Marketing Playbook

**Market:** Sierra Leone (Freetown first) · **Niche:** food sellers · **Budget:** $0 · **Time:** 5–10 hrs/week · **Goal:** 30 active catalogs by day 90

---

## The thesis

You are a solo, remote founder marketing into a WhatsApp-first market. You cannot win with ads (no budget), cold content (no audience), or feet on the street (you're in the Philippines). You *can* win with three assets you already have:

1. **Your SL contacts** — converted into 2–3 *founding sellers* whose real catalogs become your demo, your screenshots, and your credibility.
2. **The built-in viral loop** — every free catalog shows a "Made with Cataloo" footer link, and every free PDF export carries a watermark + clickable link. Every customer who receives a catalog sees Cataloo. Your sellers are your distribution.
3. **Concierge onboarding** — you build catalogs *for* sellers. A seller who sends you 10 photos on WhatsApp and gets a live catalog back in 24 hours never hits the "empty dashboard" wall. Activation is your job, not theirs.

One number matters: **active catalogs** — a catalog with 5+ items whose link was shared in the past 7 days. Signups are vanity; shared catalogs are the business.

**Timezone advantage:** Freetown is GMT+0, Philippines is GMT+8. Your 6–9 PM is Freetown's 10 AM–1 PM — prime business hours. Do all calls and outreach in your evenings.

## Positioning & messaging kit

**One-liner:**
> Cataloo turns your WhatsApp business into a professional catalog — one link your customers can browse and order from, no app, no website.

**For food sellers (primary pitch):**
> Stop re-sending the same food photos to every customer who asks "wetin you get today?" Put your whole menu on one link — photos, prices, everything. Customers browse it and their order lands in your WhatsApp. Free to start.

**For any small seller:**
> Your products deserve better than a blurry WhatsApp status that disappears in 24 hours. Cataloo gives you a permanent page — cataloo.app/yourname — that you share once and update anytime.

**For end customers (the viral-loop audience):**
> Browse the full menu, pick what you want, and your order opens in WhatsApp ready to send. No account, no app download.

**Localized angles — use these in every piece of content:**
- **"Works inside the WhatsApp you already use"** — orders arrive as normal WhatsApp messages; nothing new to learn.
- **"Light on data"** — pages load fast on a small data bundle; customers don't need to download anything.
- **"Look bigger than you are"** — a clean catalog with categories and search reads like an established business, not a hustle.
- **"Your status disappears. Your catalog doesn't."** — the sharpest contrast with how sellers currently work.
- **vs. WhatsApp Business catalog:** your Cataloo link works for people who *don't* have your number saved, has search and categories, exports to PDF, and can be posted anywhere — Facebook, flyers, bio links.

## Phase 1 — Foundations (Days 1–14)

**Outcome by day 14:** 3 founding sellers live and sharing, 1 polished demo catalog, brand accounts created, first 4 pieces of content posted.

### 1. Build the demo catalog yourself (Day 1–2, ~2 hrs)
Before you have users, you need something to show. Create a realistic Freetown food business catalog under a demo handle — e.g. a home kitchen with 15–20 items: cassava leaves, jollof, groundnut soup, fry fry, cakes, drinks. Use appetizing, realistic photos and Leone pricing. This is the link you drop in every DM, post, and group. It must look so good a seller says "I want that."

### 2. Recruit 3 founding sellers from your SL contacts (Days 1–10)

Send this to Abi Jaoudi and every SL contact you have:

> Hi [Name]! I built something I think could be big in Salone and I want your help to prove it. It's called Cataloo — it gives any small business a professional product catalog on one link, and customers order straight through WhatsApp. Here's a demo: [demo link]
>
> I'm looking for 3 businesses to be founding users. I'll build their entire catalog for them, free, and give them Pro features free for 6 months. All they do is send me photos + prices on WhatsApp and then share their link with customers.
>
> Do you know any food sellers — caterers, home cooks, bakers — who'd want this? Even one intro would help me a lot.

When a seller says yes, send:

> Great! Here's all I need from you on WhatsApp: (1) your business name, (2) a short line about what you sell, (3) photos of your products — even phone photos are fine, (4) the price for each, (5) the WhatsApp number where orders should come. Send them anytime — your catalog will be live within 24 hours.

### 3. Concierge onboarding checklist (repeat for every seller, ever)
- [ ] Create their account and handle (their business name, clean spelling)
- [ ] Upload items with categories (e.g. Mains / Snacks / Drinks) — categories make catalogs look professional
- [ ] Set the WhatsApp order number and business details
- [ ] Send them the live link with: "This is yours: cataloo.app/[handle]. Try ordering from it yourself to see what your customers see."
- [ ] Walk them through posting it: WhatsApp status, WhatsApp group they sell in, Facebook page/profile
- [ ] 48 hours later, ask: "Did anyone order through the link yet? Any confusion?" — write down every answer; this is your market research
- [ ] Day 7: the referral ask (script in Phase 2)

### 4. Set up brand presence (Days 3–7, ~2 hrs)
- **Facebook page "Cataloo"** — Facebook is the dominant social network in Sierra Leone; this is your main channel. Bio: the one-liner + demo link.
- **TikTok "@cataloo.app"** — for faceless screen-recording demos.
- **WhatsApp Business number for Cataloo** — the "contact us" and support channel; also lets you demo the product's own medium.
- Skip Instagram, X, and LinkedIn for now. Two channels done weekly beats five done badly.

### 5. First content (Days 7–14)
Post these four pieces to seed both channels:
1. **Screen recording (TikTok + FB):** building a full food catalog from nothing in under 5 minutes, sped up, text overlay: "Your WhatsApp food business deserves a menu. Watch me build one in 5 minutes — free."
2. **Written FB post:** the "your status disappears, your catalog doesn't" angle, with demo link.
3. **Before/after image post:** left, a blurry WhatsApp status collage; right, the demo catalog. Caption: "Same products. Which business would you order from?"
4. **Founding seller announcement:** screenshot of the first real catalog, tag/name the business: "[Business] is now taking orders on one link."

## Phase 2 — Seed & learn (Days 15–45)

**Outcome by day 45:** 25 signups, 15 activated catalogs (5+ items), 10 shared within the past week. You know which channel produces sellers who actually share.

### 1. The free-build offer, at volume
Your core growth motion: find food sellers, offer to build their catalog free. Target: **10 concierge builds** in this phase (≈2–3/week, ~45 min each).

**Where to find them:**
- Ask each founding seller for 2 intros (script below) — warm beats cold every time.
- Facebook: search and join groups using terms like *"Sierra Leone business"*, *"Freetown buy and sell"*, *"Salone market"*, *"Sierra Leone entrepreneurs"*, *"Freetown food"*. Join 5+; note which are active (daily posts) vs. dead.
- Facebook search for food sellers posting products directly: search *"available in Freetown"*, browse food hashtags, check who's selling in group comments.
- SL diaspora groups (UK/US) — diaspora members often order food deliveries for family back home; sellers serving them are ideal early adopters.

**Referral ask (send to every seller 7 days after their catalog goes live):**

> Quick favor — you know other people selling food or products on WhatsApp. If you send me 2 names or drop my number in a sellers' group you're in, I'll build their catalogs free too, same as yours. The more sellers using this, the more customers get used to ordering this way — it helps your business too.

**Cold DM to a seller found in a group (never post-and-run; DM after engaging with their post):**

> Hi! I saw your [dish/product] in [group name] — looks great. I run Cataloo, a free tool that puts your whole menu on one link customers can browse and order from through WhatsApp. Here's what it looks like: [best real seller catalog link]
>
> I'm building catalogs free for the first food sellers in Freetown — you send me photos + prices, I send you back your own page within a day. No cost, no catch — I'm growing the platform and your catalog is my advertising. Want one?

**Group post (only in groups that allow promo posts — read rules first; 1 post per group per 2 weeks max):**

> FREE for Freetown food sellers 🍲 I'm building professional online menus for the first 10 food businesses that reply — normally this is what people pay web designers for. One link with all your food, photos and prices; customers browse and order straight to your WhatsApp. Real example: [seller catalog link]. Comment "menu" or DM me and I'll build yours within 24 hours.

### 2. Weekly content cadence (~3 hrs/week, all faceless)
- **2 written FB posts/week.** Rotate: seller spotlight (screenshot + their story), a tip ("3 photo tricks that make food sell on WhatsApp"), an angle post (data-light, status-vs-catalog, look-professional), an order-flow demo ("this is what your customer sees").
- **1 screen recording/week (TikTok + FB Reels).** Rotate: speed-build of a real seller's catalog (with permission), "how a customer orders in 30 seconds", one feature (search, categories, PDF export), before/after transformations.
- Batch everything in one weekly session. Reuse each seller's catalog as content — every concierge build is also a post.

### 3. Learn deliberately
Keep a one-line note per seller: where they came from, did they activate, did they share, what they asked, what confused them. By day 45 you should be able to answer: *Which source produces sellers who actually share their link?* That answer decides Phase 3.

## Phase 3 — Amplify (Days 46–90)

**Outcome by day 90:** 60+ signups, 30 active catalogs, 2–3 ambassadors onboarding sellers without you, 1 published case study, one clear winning channel getting ~60% of your time.

### 1. Ambassador program
Pick your 2–3 most connected, most enthusiastic sellers (or a Freetown food blogger / foodie page admin). Offer:

> You've seen what Cataloo does for your business. I want to make you a Cataloo ambassador: you get Pro free permanently — unlimited items, multiple photos, your own branding, analytics. In return, you help other sellers get started: show them your catalog, send them to me (or set them up yourself — it takes 10 minutes). For every 5 sellers you bring on who publish a catalog, I'll also feature your business across all Cataloo pages and posts for a month.

Ambassadors are your feet on the street. This is how a remote founder gets local presence for free.

### 2. The case study
Take your best founding seller and write the story: who they are, how they sold before (status posts, repeated DMs), what changed (orders via the link, time saved, customers they couldn't reach before), one strong quote, before/after visuals. Publish it as: a FB post, a TikTok slideshow, the testimonial on the landing page, and the anchor for press pitches.

### 3. Light PR & community (only after the case study exists)
- **Freetown tech community:** Sensi Tech Hub and Freetown Pitch Night — reach out, ask to demo or be featured; a local tech community co-sign matters in a small market.
- **Pan-African tech media:** Disrupt Africa, TechCabal, Techpoint Africa. Pitch email:

> Subject: Freetown food sellers are replacing status posts with one-link catalogs
>
> Hi [Name], I'm the founder of Cataloo (cataloo.app), a free tool that gives WhatsApp-based sellers a professional catalog on a single link — customers browse and order straight into the seller's WhatsApp. In [X] weeks, [N] food sellers in Freetown have published catalogs, like [seller]: [link]. WhatsApp commerce is huge across West Africa but sellers still work from disappearing status posts — we think the catalog link becomes the storefront. Happy to share seller stories, numbers, and what we're learning about social commerce in Sierra Leone. Worth a short piece?

- **A Cataloo WhatsApp community/channel** for your sellers: tips, feature news, and peer proof. Your retention channel — an active seller community is also your churn defense.

### 4. Double down
Whatever your day-45 review says is the winning channel — founder referrals, one specific FB group, TikTok demos — give it 60% of your hours. Kill anything that produced zero activated sellers in 30 days. Do *not* add new channels in this phase.

## Weekly operating rhythm (fits 5–10 hrs)

| Day (PH time) | Block | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon evening | Outreach: DMs, group engagement, referral asks (= Freetown late morning) | 1.5 h |
| Wed evening | Concierge build #1 + follow-ups with recent sellers | 1.5 h |
| Fri evening | Concierge build #2 + seller check-ins | 1.5 h |
| Sat afternoon | Content batch: 2 posts + 1 screen recording, schedule for the week | 2.5 h |
| Sun | 20-min review: update tracker, pick next week's targets | 0.5 h |

Ratio ≈ outreach 3 : onboarding 3 : content 2. If you only have 5 hours in a week, cut content first — never cut onboarding.

**The tracker (one Google Sheet, one row per seller):**
`Name · WhatsApp # · Source (contact / referral / FB group name / TikTok / badge) · Signup date · Items count · Concierge? · First share date · Shared this week? · Notes`

## Metrics & checkpoints

**Definitions:** *Activated* = catalog with 5+ items. *Active* = activated + link shared in past 7 days (self-reported + catalog page views you can check server-side — user-facing analytics is Pro-only, but you own the database).

| Checkpoint | Targets | If missed |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 14** | 3 founding catalogs live · demo catalog polished · FB + TikTok + WhatsApp Business live · 4 content pieces posted | Founding sellers are non-negotiable — widen the contact ask before doing anything else |
| **Day 45** | 25 signups · 15 activated · 10 shared in past week · 10 concierge builds done · content cadence held 4 straight weeks | See pivot triggers |
| **Day 90** | 60+ signups · 30 active catalogs · 2–3 ambassadors · 1 case study published · ≥20% of signups from badge/referral | Diagnose: awareness problem (few signups) vs. value problem (signups but no sharing) — they have opposite fixes |

**Pivot triggers:**
- **Concierge-built catalogs aren't being shared (<50% by day 45).** This is a value problem, not a marketing problem. Stop recruiting; interview every seller: what would make this worth sharing weekly? Fix that first.
- **Food sellers sign up but stall; other verticals activate better.** Follow the pull — fashion/thrift resellers are the likely second wedge; the playbook is identical.
- **FB groups produce nothing but founder referrals convert.** Go all-in on the referral chain and ambassadors; drop group posting.
- **Signups fine, all from PH/elsewhere, not SL.** Your content is finding the wrong geography — shift weight from TikTok to SL-specific FB groups and ambassador ground game.

## Product asks that serve marketing (small dev tasks, this repo)

1. **Trackable viral-loop links.** The free-catalog badge (`app/[handle]/page.tsx`) and the PDF footer link (`components/catalog/ExportButton.tsx`) both point to bare `https://cataloo.app`. Change to `https://cataloo.app?ref=badge` and `?ref=pdf`, and log `ref` on signup — without this you can't measure the loop (day-90 target: ≥20% of signups from it).
2. **WhatsApp share button in the dashboard.** There's a copy-link button (`app/dashboard/page.tsx`) but no one-tap "Share on WhatsApp" with pre-filled text ("Browse my full menu and order here 👉🏾 [link]"). Sharing is the metric that matters — make it one tap. Add the same to a "share your catalog" nudge after a seller adds their 5th item.
3. **Social-proof section on the landing page.** Once founding sellers are live: 2–3 real catalog screenshots + one quote. Replace abstract promises with real Freetown businesses.
4. **Signup source tracking.** Persist `ref`/`utm_source` from the landing URL through the signup flow into the profile row — this makes the whole tracker measurable instead of self-reported.
